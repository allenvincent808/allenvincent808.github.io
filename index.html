About

I am a PhD candidate in chemistry at Iowa State University in Prof. Rui Sun's group. I started my PhD at the University of Hawaiʻi at Mānoa and moved with the group to Iowa State in Fall 2026. I build potential energy surfaces with electronic structure methods, then run ab initio dynamics on them to predict which products form, how fast and with what energy.

Most of my projects are joint work with experimental groups in the U.S. and Germany. Their crossed-beam and ion–molecule measurements test my simulations, and my simulations explain what their instruments see.

Outside research, I founded and led the student leadership council of an NSF Engineering Research Center across six universities, and I have taught chemistry and mathematics for more than six years.

Atmospheric chemistryPCCP, 2026Lead authorPacifichem 2025 talk

Does a common refrigerant really form TFA in the atmosphere?

Problem
Trifluoroacetic acid (TFA) is a persistent fluorinated pollutant. Earlier work proposed gas-phase pathways by which HFC-134a, a widely used refrigerant, could form it.
Approach
Mapped the energetics of the proposed pathways with quantum chemistry, then simulated them at tropospheric temperatures and pressures.
Result
The energy barriers on those routes are out of reach under atmospheric conditions, and the intermediates are stabilized instead. The proposed pathways do not produce TFA.
Next
Extending the approach to HFO-1234yf, the newer low-GWP refrigerant, and its route to trifluoroacetaldehyde.

Organosilicon chemistryScience Advances, 2024Co-first author

A counterintuitive route to silylacetylene in the gas phase

Problem
Explain how silylacetylene (SiH₃CCH) forms from the ethynyl radical and silane in crossed molecular beam experiments.
Approach
Electronic structure calculations and ab initio dynamics, matched to the measured product distributions.
Result
The product forms through an unexpected insertion of the ethynyl radical, a mechanism relevant to silicon–carbon chemistry.

Ion–molecule reactionsPCCP, 2024Co-first author

Ion–molecule reactions of HBr⁺ with methane

Problem
Identify the reaction channels behind the products seen in ion–molecule experiments on HBr⁺ + CH₄.
Approach
Direct dynamics simulations compared with experiments from collaborators at the University of Marburg.
Result
A combined experimental and theoretical account of the reaction mechanisms.
